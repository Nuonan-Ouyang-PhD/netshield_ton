\section{Theorem Derivations}
For $m$ remotely represented probes and $k$ full-evidence probes, payload is
$mS+k(B-S)$. Hence one-round feasibility is
\[kB+(m-k)S\le C,\]
and, for $C\ge mS$,
\[k^*(C,m)=\min\left\{m,\left\lfloor\frac{C-mS}{B-S}\right\rfloor\right\}.\]
With $\rho=C/(nB)$, $\sigma=S/B$, $r=m/n$, and $f=k/n$, the continuous
frontier is $\sigma r+(1-\sigma)f\le\rho$.

If non-selected probes may send zero remote payload, payload for $k$ full
requests is minimized by setting $m=k$, so the unrestricted full-evidence
byte frontier is
\[K_{\rm full}^{\max}=\min\{n,\lfloor C/B\rfloor\}.\]
Provided $C\geq nS$, requiring all probes to remain remotely represented gives
\[K_{\rm all}^{\max}=\left[\left\lfloor\frac{C-nS}{B-S}\right\rfloor\right]_0^n.\]
If $C<nS$, the all-summary baseline is itself infeasible; clipping the count
to zero does not make universal coverage feasible.
For the formal $n=3$, $B=128$~KiB, $S=16$~KiB system, unrestricted versus
all-probe counts are $(1,0)$, $(2,1)$, and $(3,3)$ at low, mid, and high
capacity. Thus universal per-round coverage costs one full-evidence slot at
low and mid capacity.

For total admitted bytes $A_t$ released at the start of round $t$, an
idealized preemptive fluid server with common relative deadline $D$ rounds
satisfies the demand condition
\[\sum_{\tau=s}^{t}A_\tau\le C(t-s+D),\qquad s\le t.\]
$D=1$ reduces to per-round feasibility. Dividing the $s=1$ case by $T$ gives
the long-run load condition
\[\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}A_t\leq C.\]
The latter is a load-admissibility condition, not a deterministic bounded-
delay guarantee for arbitrary burst processes.

For synchronized fixed-full overload from empty queues with $nB>C$,
work-conserving service removes $\min\{Q_\Sigma(t)+nB,C\}=C$ per round,
giving $Q_\Sigma(T)=T(nB-C)$. At mid capacity, the post-full per-probe queue
is $B/3=43{,}690.\overline{6}$~B, or approximately 42.7~KiB. This exceeds
every positive-payload threshold; the next H-NONE round drains the residual,
producing the frozen I-QPG full/none alternation.

In the reduced H-FULL/H-NONE desynchronized regime, if aggregate backlog is
rate-stable and the server is asymptotically saturated, flow conservation
gives
\[p^*=\min(1,C/(nB))=\min(1,\rho).\]
The phase-randomized software study matches $1/3$, $2/3$, and $1$ at the
three capacities.

Finally, with $K=K_{\rm full}^{\max}$, selecting probes
$\mathcal S_t=\{(tK+j)\bmod n:j=0,\ldots,K-1\}$ attains the unrestricted
byte frontier every round. Its period is $n/\gcd(n,K)$, and each probe is
selected $K/\gcd(n,K)$ times per period.


\section{Analytical Extensions for Deployment}
\label{sec:supp_deployment}
These extensions derive planning consequences of the one-round byte model.
They are not new hardware measurements, fitted arrival models, or guarantees
of physical deadline attainment. MB denotes $10^6$ bytes; KiB and MiB denote
$2^{10}$ and $2^{20}$ bytes. The integer boundaries remain the operational
reference for the three-probe measurements.

\subsection{Probabilistic Requests and Capacity Planning}
\label{sec:supp_probabilistic}
Assume empty incoming queues, fixed $0<S<B$, and a budget $C\geq nS$ that
can first reserve summaries for all $n$ probes. Within a round, each probe
independently requests a full upload with common probability $p\in[0,1]$;
otherwise it requests a summary. Independence across rounds is not needed
for the following marginal per-round calculation. Let
\begin{equation}
J\sim\operatorname{Binomial}(n,p),\qquad
h(C)=\min\left\{n,\left\lfloor\frac{C-nS}{B-S}\right\rfloor\right\}.
\label{eq:supp_h}
\end{equation}
Under a count-maximizing summary-preserving admission rule, the admitted
full count is $J_{\rm adm}=\min\{J,h(C)\}$. Consequently,
\begin{align}
P_{\rm deg}(C)
&=\Pr\{J>h(C)\}
=\sum_{j=h(C)+1}^{n}\binom{n}{j}p^j(1-p)^{n-j},
\label{eq:supp_pdeg}\\
\mathbb E[J_{\rm adm}]
&=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\min\{j,h(C)\}\binom{n}{j}p^j(1-p)^{n-j}.
\label{eq:supp_expected_admission}
\end{align}
Here $P_{\rm deg}$ is the probability that a round needs at least one
full-to-summary downgrade, not a per-request loss probability. The expected
number of downgraded requests is $np-\mathbb E[J_{\rm adm}]$. Non-requesting
and downgraded probes still retain summary evidence. Both equations concern
admitted bytes, not returned core results.

For a target $\epsilon\in[0,1)$, define the upper-tail quantile
\begin{equation}
q_\epsilon=\min\{q\in\{0,\ldots,n\}:\Pr(J>q)\leq\epsilon\}.
\label{eq:supp_quantile}
\end{equation}
The minimum budget within this model that preserves all summaries and
satisfies $P_{\rm deg}\leq\epsilon$ is
\begin{equation}
\boxed{C_\epsilon=nS+(B-S)q_\epsilon.}
\label{eq:supp_capacity}
\end{equation}
Indeed, $h(C_\epsilon)=q_\epsilon$. Any smaller budget either fails the
summary baseline (when $q_\epsilon=0$) or admits fewer than $q_\epsilon$ full
requests, contradicting quantile minimality. Since $h(C)$ is non-decreasing,
expected admitted richness is also non-decreasing in $C$.

\paragraph{Illustrative calculation, not an empirical result}
With the paper's $n=3$, $B=131{,}072$~B and $S=16{,}384$~B, suppose
independently $p=2/3$ and choose $\epsilon=1/3$. Then
$\Pr(J>1)=20/27$ but $\Pr(J>2)=8/27$, giving $q_\epsilon=2$ and
$C_\epsilon=278{,}528$~B (272~KiB). At this budget,
$\mathbb E[J_{\rm adm}]=46/27$ and the expected downgrade count is $8/27$
per round. The planning budget differs from the hardware mid budget of
256~KiB because it reserves a summary for the third probe. The numerical
choice $p=2/3$ does not establish independence of the QPG traces or identify
this planning rule with CRFA, which assigns H-NONE to non-selected probes.

For a large-$n$ interpretation, hold $B,S,p$ and normalized capacity
$\rho=C_n/(nB)$ fixed, with $\rho\geq\sigma=S/B$. Then
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathbb E[J_{\rm adm}]}{n}
\longrightarrow
\min\left\{p,\min\left(1,\frac{\rho-\sigma}{1-\sigma}\right)\right\}.
\label{eq:supp_large_n}
\end{equation}
This follows because $J/n\to p$ in probability, $h(C_n)/n$ converges to the
clipped capacity term, and the normalized admitted count is bounded by one.
For $\rho<\sigma$, universal summary coverage is infeasible rather than a
zero-richness feasible point. With correlated within-round requests, the
binomial tail is not justified; the same quantile construction requires the
actual joint request-count distribution. Queueing, security utility, and
physical timing remain outside this planning calculation.

\subsection{Heterogeneous Payloads: Summary Baseline and Upgrade Costs}
\label{sec:supp_heterogeneous}
Fix the set $\mathcal M$ of probes required to retain remote evidence, with
$0<S_i<B_i$ for every $i\in\mathcal M$. Let
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathcal M}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal M}S_i,\quad
d_i=B_i-S_i>0,\quad R=C-S_{\mathcal M}.
\label{eq:supp_hetero_baseline}
\end{equation}
If $R<0$, the prescribed coverage set is infeasible. Otherwise, upgrading
$\mathcal F\subseteq\mathcal M$ to full evidence is feasible exactly when
\begin{equation}
S_{\mathcal M}+\sum_{i\in\mathcal F}d_i\leq C.
\label{eq:supp_hetero_constraint}
\end{equation}
For a pure full-count objective (equal positive value per upgrade), sort
$d_{(1)}\leq\cdots\leq d_{(m)}$, where $m=|\mathcal M|$. The optimum is
\begin{equation}
k_{\rm het}^{\star}
=\max\left\{k\in\{0,\ldots,m\}:\sum_{j=1}^{k}d_{(j)}\leq R\right\}.
\label{eq:supp_hetero_count}
\end{equation}
Any set of $k$ upgrades costs at least the sum of the $k$ smallest costs,
which proves feasibility and optimality of this count rule. When payloads
are homogeneous, it reduces to
$\min\{m,\lfloor(C-mS)/(B-S)\rfloor\}$ for $C\geq mS$.

For general nonnegative values $w_i$, the formulation is instead
\begin{equation}
\max_{x_i\in\{0,1\}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal M}w_i x_i
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
\sum_{i\in\mathcal M}d_i x_i\leq R,
\label{eq:supp_knapsack}
\end{equation}
a 0/1 knapsack problem. Equal value is not equal value density. With
$R=3$ and cost/value pairs $(2,2)$ and $(3,3)$, both densities equal one,
but choosing the cheaper upgrade yields value 2 while choosing the other
yields value 3. Thus equal density does not justify an increasing-cost greedy
rule for the weighted problem. No heterogeneous-payload hardware validation
or security-value optimum is claimed here; the scope is a fixed coverage set
with separable values and no additional fairness constraints.

\subsection{Continuous Exchange Rate versus Discrete Feasibility}
\label{sec:supp_continuous}
In the continuous relaxation, $0\leq f\leq r\leq1$ and
$\sigma r+(1-\sigma)f\leq\rho$. Along any non-degenerate feasible segment
of the active budget line,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm dr}{\mathrm df}=-\frac{1-\sigma}{\sigma}.
\label{eq:supp_exchange_rate}
\end{equation}
This is a local coverage--richness exchange rate, not an integer scheduling
rule. With $\sigma=1/8$, its magnitude is seven. The full-coverage boundary
point $r=1$, $f=(\rho-\sigma)/(1-\sigma)$ applies only for
$\sigma\leq\rho\leq1$; for $\rho>1$, $(r,f)=(1,1)$ is already maximal
although the byte budget is slack.

Discrete Pareto points can likewise leave unused capacity. At $n=3$ and
$C=B$ with $S=B/8$, the all-summary allocation $(m,k)=(3,0)$ costs only
$3S=3B/8$. It nevertheless cannot increase $k$ while retaining $m=3$,
since one full upload plus two summaries costs $B+2S>B$. It is therefore
undominated in the integer coverage/count ordering despite its slack byte
budget. For the hardware testbed, the integer slot price and CRFA selection
schedule, rather than a continuous Pareto assertion, govern the feasible
operating points.

\section{Independence Approximation}
At mid capacity, an independent Bernoulli request model with $p=2/3$ gives
$P(J=1)=6/27$, $P(J=2)=12/27$, and $P(J=3)=8/27$. If a 2$B$ service budget
finishes all requests for $J\leq2$ and none for $J=3$, the expected conditional
timely fraction is $5/9\approx0.5556$ and the common timely-full fraction is
$10/27\approx0.3704$. The phase-randomized simulator gives 0.5028 and 0.3352,
respectively. The Bernoulli calculation is an independence approximation, not
a proved upper bound.

\section{CRFA Hardware Frontier Extension}
The separately frozen CRFA campaign tests the same cyclic construction on the
three physical probes without changing the primary A--F campaign. Twelve
matched session/workload identities are executed at each capacity, yielding
36 accepted arms. Across 55 indexed attempts, 19 attempts are excluded for
predefined infrastructure failures; all excluded attempts are retained in the
attempt index, timing outcomes are never acceptance criteria, and partial
attempts are not stitched into accepted arms. The accepted extension contains
9,720 measured occurrences and 10,620 reconstructed shared-service rounds.
All frozen event-identity, action/payload, final-drain, raw-reconstruction,
and shared-cap gates pass, with zero shared-cap violations.

\section{Detector and Replay Limitations}
The workload is frozen CICIoT2023-derived feature-vector replay, not packet replay or an uncontrolled deployment. The dataset is cited in the main paper. The formal occurrence set excludes Recon-PortScan, and exact feature-vector overlap exists across frozen partitions. Perfect correctness on the formal occurrences is therefore a controlled diagnostic rather than an IDS-superiority result.

\section{Raw Audit and Offline-Analysis Verdicts}
The primary full-raw audit rebuilt 216/216 accepted arms and 58,320 measured
events with zero raw-to-metric mismatch and zero shared-cap violation across
63,720 service rounds. Its historical verdict remains
\texttt{PASS\_WITH\_ISSUES} because the raw bundle lacks direct artifacts
for the S03-a01 SSD incident and the S07 pre-hardware launcher abort; the
CRFA extension does not alter that disclosure.

The separately archived CRFA evidence reconstructs 36/36 accepted arms,
9,720 measured occurrences, and 10,620 shared-service rounds. Its frozen
identity, action/payload, final-drain, reconstruction, and cap gates pass, and
no shared-cap violation is observed. Across the two hardware campaigns this
corresponds to 252 accepted arms, 68,040 measured occurrences, and 74,340
reconstructed shared-service rounds, with zero observed shared-cap violations.

The read-only offline analyses contain 432 queue-weight rows, 240 phase-
sensitivity rows, 648 primary schedule-anchor rows, and 20 multi-$n$ scaling
rows. The rotating software-conformance table remains a separate discrete-
event check and is not relabelled as hardware evidence.
